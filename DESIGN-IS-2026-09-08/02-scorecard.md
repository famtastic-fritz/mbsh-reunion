# Dieter Rams scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: The role-aware Event Cinema workspace refreshes conventional portals with contextual guidance, but Harry is still a rule-based router ([evidence](01-evidence.md#1-innovative)).
   Justification: It improves an established admin pattern without introducing a demonstrably new interaction category.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Five prominent tasks work, but the primary Menu fails and dashboard metrics do not open their promised workspaces ([evidence](01-evidence.md#2-useful)).
   Justification: Core tasks exist, but users must detour around broken or inert navigation.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: The cinematic visual system is coherent, but mobile overflows by 48 px, help overlays cover content, and the hidden permission control is visible ([evidence](01-evidence.md#3-aesthetic)).
   Justification: Multiple inconsistencies include at least one jarring mobile-layout violation.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Concrete section labels coexist with broken Menu behavior, duplicate route identities, duplicate ticket panels, and operator jargon ([evidence](01-evidence.md#4-understandable)).
   Justification: More than three controls or concepts require explanation or behave differently from their labels.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Multiple guides, a persistent helper, and three tour launchers compete with operational work ([evidence](01-evidence.md#5-unobtrusive)).
   Justification: Instructional chrome is prominent enough to compete with content.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 0/3
   Evidence: The UI claims a ticket is ready, the attendee is verified, RSVP changes are saved, and email/SMS systems are connected before authoritative success exists ([evidence](01-evidence.md#6-honest)).
   Justification: False entitlement and saved-state claims are deceptive even if accidental.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: The durable cinematic identity is undermined by hard-coded "Tonight," event phase, date, and localhost assumptions ([evidence](01-evidence.md#7-long-lasting)).
   Justification: Several time-bound markers require code changes as the reunion moves between acts.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Many states and backend edge cases exist, but interaction tests miss the broken Menu and live committee additions are outside version control ([evidence](01-evidence.md#8-thorough)).
   Justification: The state vocabulary is strong, yet critical production interaction and parity gaps remain.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Initial JS is below 100 KB, idle animation is absent, dark presentation is native, and reduced motion is respected ([evidence](01-evidence.md#9-environmentally-friendly)).
   Justification: It meets the rubric's strongest software-resource signals despite an eager API boot.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Dozens of controls, repeated tasks, duplicate ticket panels, and three tour affordances leave multiple removable elements ([evidence](01-evidence.md#10-as-little-design-as-possible)).
   Justification: At least three redundant elements can be removed without harming the primary task.

**Total: 12/30**
