# Hi-Tide Harry Reunion Campaign Creative Recipe

**Recorded:** August 17, 2026  
**Project:** MBSH Class of 1996 — 30th Reunion / Miami Beach Senior High School 100th anniversary  
**Purpose:** Reproduce the approved cinematic reunion ads without losing character, event, or brand consistency.

## What produced the approved work

The strongest stills were generated with an image-generation workflow using the approved Hi-Tide Harry character, reunion medallion, black/red/gold premiere palette, and explicit composition constraints. OpenArt/Kling then animated an approved still into the nine-second character teaser. Adobe tools are the controlled finishing layer for cleanup, typography, resizing, color consistency, and export; they were not the original generator for these particular stills. Remotion is the repeatable option for deterministic titles, captions, aspect-ratio variants, and campaign packaging.

## Canonical visual anchors

- Hi-Tide Harry: red skin, tall swept forelock, pointed ears, large friendly eyes, white Hi-Tide Harry shirt, red pants, black shoes with red/white soles, optional silver cape.
- Medallion: `30 YEARS / 1996 2026 / 100 YEARS`, silver outer ring, red center, three wave marks.
- Art direction: theatrical premiere, deep red velvet, black marble or dark cinema interiors, warm gold practical lighting, restrained sea reflections.
- Event truth: Class of 1996, 30th reunion, November 7, 2026, and the school's 100-year milestone.
- Tone: mysterious, premium, nostalgic, celebratory, and welcoming—not childish clip art.

## Repeatable production workflow

1. Select one approved Harry image as the character reference. Do not regenerate from text alone when consistency matters.
2. Lock the event facts and medallion wording before visual generation.
3. Generate the scene without relying on AI-rendered body copy. Reserve a clean text-safe zone.
4. Compare Harry's face, forelock, ears, clothing, proportions, and colors with the reference before accepting the image.
5. Add the final headline, date, CTA, and logo/medallion in Photoshop, Adobe Express, or Remotion.
6. Export platform variants from the approved master: square, portrait feed, vertical story/reel, and landscape/video poster.
7. Animate only an approved still. Keep motion purposeful: entrance, cape movement, medallion reveal, theater lights, or camera push—not random morphing.
8. Run visual, factual, mobile-crop, accessibility, and rights QA before publishing.
9. Store the approved output, reference asset, prompt/version notes, and date together. Never overwrite the approved master.

## Base image prompt template

> Create a premium ultra-photoreal cinematic reunion campaign scene featuring the approved Hi-Tide Harry character reference. Preserve his exact recognizable face, tall red forelock, pointed ears, friendly large eyes, white Hi-Tide Harry shirt, red pants, black shoes, and optional silver cape. Place him in a luxurious dark movie-premiere environment with deep red velvet, black architectural detail, warm gold practical lighting, subtle ocean reflections, and the approved 1996–2026 reunion medallion. The mood is mysterious, nostalgic, triumphant, and welcoming. Compose for [FORMAT] with Harry inside the safe crop and reserve [TEXT ZONE] for controlled typography. No extra characters, duplicate limbs, altered medallion wording, random logos, illegible text, cartoon-flat rendering, or cropped face/hands.

Scene modifiers used across the series included theater entrance, countdown vault, illuminated medallion, calling-all-Hi-Tides rally, megaphone pose, mythic guardian, living-fantasy character, practical-effects mascot, and cinematic animatronic interpretation.

## Still-to-video recipe

1. Start with the highest-resolution approved portrait still.
2. In OpenArt/Kling, request one continuous 8–10 second cinematic movement: Harry enters or leans forward, cape and fabric respond naturally, lights rise, the medallion catches a controlled gold highlight, and the camera makes a slow push.
3. Lock identity and forbid face reshaping, new text, extra fingers/limbs, clothing changes, medallion changes, or background cuts.
4. Export a clean master without generated captions.
5. Use Remotion, Premiere, or Adobe Express for deterministic titles, captions, music, CTA end card, and 9:16/4:5/1:1 versions.
6. Inspect every cut at phone size. Text and faces must remain within safe zones; audio must not autoplay on the website.

## Export specifications

| Use | Preferred canvas | Safe-area rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram/Facebook square | 1080 × 1080 | Keep critical content inside central 900 × 900 |
| Instagram/Facebook feed | 1080 × 1350 (4:5) | Keep face, medallion, and CTA away from top/bottom 120 px |
| Stories/Reels/TikTok/Shorts | 1080 × 1920 (9:16) | Keep critical text between roughly y=250 and y=1600 |
| Web/video poster | 1920 × 1080 or source-native landscape | Provide a mobile portrait crop or art-directed fallback |

## Required QA

- Spell and verify every date, class year, anniversary, CTA, URL, and name.
- Distinguish the **Class of 1996** from the **1995 junior-year yearbook** source material.
- Confirm no text, face, hands, shoes, medallion, or CTA is clipped in every delivery crop.
- Keep essential text in HTML or controlled overlays; AI-generated lettering is not authoritative.
- Add descriptive alt text and avoid using imagery as the only carrier of essential information.
- Preserve reduced-motion and poster fallbacks for web use.
- Do not publish raw diploma photos, yearbook names/faces, signatures, or identifying records without rights and consent review.
- The commissioned reunion medallion is an approved Fritz-owned project asset; preserve its exact design.
